One data note, now resolved, and new results from a second query wave run today. The archived March query log looked incoherent (the *seem* adjective query showed 72 hits with the noun exclusion but 48 without), so I re-ran everything and extended the sweeps. The four exclusion-style queries reproduce the log exactly (107, 72, 54, 63), and the degree-modified sets (111 and 268 tokens) are now checked token-complete as well: still zero genuine adjectival or adverbial nuclei with *seem*/*appear*. The unexcluded queries return 508 and 224 tokens; the March "130" and "48" were evidently counts of examined list entries, and 130 + 48 is where the "178" that both drafts carried came from. A heads-up for your manuscript: §5.6's "zero hits, compared to 178 hits for attributional verbs" will want restating, since 178 was an artefact of that list-row counting on our side; the working paper's figures are the ones to cite. Three other March figures were also off: *regard as* returns 117 (not 36), *describe as* 161 (not 32), and *take to be* has roughly fifty genuine attributional tokens, not the "about 15" the note carried, which strengthens the argument that the copula isn't what blocks category transmission.

Three of the new results bear directly on your analysis, so please look at §2 of the attached paper. First, the PP sweep found one genuine PP-nucleus evidential TFR: "He's bleeding, what appears to be from his neck" (2009), in supplement position, deletion-passing, no nominal reading available. It's a single token, and supplements invite a parenthetical reanalysis, but as it stands it's a counterexample to the evidential daughter's NP-only specification, so you'll want to decide whether the hierarchy absorbs it or the restriction weakens. Second, *turn out to be* produced the campaign's one token in a category-discriminating position: "the (what turned out to be necessary) surgery", attributive between determiner and noun. *Turn out* isn't in your §5.6 inventory; its outcome semantics doesn't fit the evidential profile, and the token shows category transmission. Third, *happen to be* behaves exactly as your classification predicts: nothing but standard free relatives in its adjective frame. One descriptive bonus: external positions differ sharply by verb (*call* 30% predicative in the census against about 1/100 for *regard as* and 3/200 for *consider*), with *call*'s skew plausibly driven by the spoken exclamative formula "that's what I call X". The census also surfaced two *call* TFRs in attributive position ("fitted-stone (what I call nibbled) masonry"; "adversarial -- and what I call stupid -- industrial relations") and one in the perfect-participle slot, so the attributional side now has naturally occurring category-discriminating tokens, not just predicative ones. And a verbatim web sweep on gradable PPs turned up clean *call*-TFR attestations for 25 distinct PP nuclei ("She's not what I would call 'with it'", "I'm not what I'd call out of shape"), mostly in a negated-predicative litotes frame; the list with sources is in the repo and a summary is in §2 of the paper.
