Thanks for this. Everything from your message is in; the new version is in the usual place: https://github.com/BrettRey/Linguistic_transparency/blob/main/main.pdf. The paper is now 33 pages.

The mechanical items first. The abstract is one paragraph, about 165 words. Line numbers now come every five lines (the modulo option you named). The stretch you flagged in the introduction is compressed by about a page: the QN, TFR, and typology previews are gone (they only rehearsed §2 and §4), and the two roadmap paragraphs are merged into one.

Lines 420 and 437 were two different tables both printing as "Table 1", one with a hand-written caption and one numbered by LaTeX. All the tables now have real captions and automatic numbers: the behavioural matrix is Table 1, the raw agreement counts Table 2, Tables 3 and 4 stay as they were, and the appendix tables are A.1 and B.1.

On §4.5, I took a middle path rather than cutting: it's now about half its old length (objection, reply, the Newmeyer response, one closing limit, with Croft demoted to a footnote). Two things made me hesitant to remove it outright. §4.1 promises the reply, and Language Sciences readers are exactly the crowd that will raise the comparative-concept objection, with Newmeyer as the canonical counter; the response to him appears nowhere else in the paper. If you'd still rather park the whole subsection and restore it only if a reviewer asks, it's an easy cut, so say the word.

On §5, you're right that the paper could stand without it, but Tables 3 and 4 live in §5.1, so cutting the section would strand the summary you liked. I kept §5.1 intact and collapsed the old §§5.2–5.4 into one short subsection (the evidential grades, the two proposed stabilizers, the three levels). The section is now about two pages, and the introduction still tells readers who only want the grammar to skip it.

Your three confirmations are all implemented as we discussed. The TFR verdict reads analysis-relative in §2.5, Table 4, and the conclusion: positive under your constructional analysis, indeterminate analysis-neutrally. The ordinary fused relative is described in §2.5 and Table 3 as a weaker, family-level counterpart, with the gate-4 strength statement spelled out. And your collectives sentence is in §2.2; while placing it I found a third leftover in §2.3 that still grouped collectives with QNs as mediated accessibility, now fixed to match.

The attached PDF is the citable form of the TFR corpus work, per your preference for a working paper. It reports the 50-hit *call* sample and the *seem*/*appear* sweeps, and it cites your indeterminacy manuscript for the two-class terminology, the CI analysis, and the inheritance hierarchy.
