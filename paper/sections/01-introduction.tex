\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}

The initial puzzle is that some grammatical relations can still access a property through a structure that appears, on the surface, to block or reverse that access. Two examples from Huddleston and Pullum's \textit{Cambridge Grammar of the English Language} (\textit{CGEL}, \citeyear{huddleston-pullum-2002}) make the point visible (§18.2, p.~502).

\ea\label{ex:cgel-two-direction}
\ea \mention{A number of spots have}/\ungram{\mention{has}} \mention{appeared.}
\ex \mention{Heaps of money has}/\ungram{\mention{have}} \mention{been spent.}
\z
\z

In the first example, the surface head \mention{number} is singular, but agreement is plural because the \mention{of}-complement \mention{spots} is plural count. In the second, the surface head \mention{heaps} is plural, but agreement is singular because the \mention{of}-complement \mention{money} is non-count. The construction lets the complement's number/count profile reach the verb, even though the surface head's own morphological number points the other way.

\textit{CGEL} calls these \term{number-transparent quantificational nouns} (QNs).\footnote{\term{Quantificational noun} is \textit{CGEL}'s label, and the paper keeps it. In the pseudo-partitive literature the same nouns are \term{quantifier nouns}, one of five sub-types alongside measure, container, part, and collection nouns \autocite[271]{van-eynde-kim-2023-pseudo-partitives}, following \textcite[109]{keizer-2007-english-noun-phrase}.} Core members include \mention{lot, plenty, lots, bags, heaps, loads, oodles, stacks, remainder, rest, number,} and \mention{couple}, with \mention{majority} and \mention{minority} at the boundary. They head NPs whose agreement override is, on \textit{CGEL}'s analysis, the default, unlike the optional override of ordinary collectives like \mention{team} and \mention{family}. The wider question is whether comparable \mention{transparency} labels pick out real accessibility relations or only restate the facts they describe. The umbrella schema is:
\begin{schemabox}
\refstepcounter{schemacounter}\label{sch:mediated-accessibility}
\textbf{Umbrella schema \theschemacounter.}\par\smallskip
$X$ is \mention{transparent} with respect to property $P$ for relation $R$ when $P$, which $X$ might otherwise have concealed from $R$, remains accessible through $X$ for the purposes of $R$.
\end{schemabox}

Applied to (\ref{ex:cgel-two-direction}a), Schema~\ref{sch:mediated-accessibility} binds its three variables to visible expressions. $X$ is the whole subject configuration \mention{a number of spots}: a number-transparent quantificational noun plus its \mention{of}-complement. $P$ is the property accessed through that configuration, the plural count profile of \mention{spots}. $R$ is the relation doing the accessing, the subject--verb agreement realized on \mention{have}. The mediator could have blocked the relation (singular \mention{number} is the surface head) and doesn't; that's what the label \mention{transparent} records.

When is such a label worth having? The useful cases are the ones where it supports prediction; call these \term{projectibility profiles}. Projectibility inherits Goodman's question of which predicates support reliable inductive projection \autocite{Goodman1955}; Boyd's maintenance-based realism supplies the stabilizing-process side \autocite{boyd1991}. A profile has three components: an \term{observable input}, the predictions that input licenses, and a \term{stabilizing process}. The QN class shows the shape concretely. The input is membership in \textit{CGEL}'s small class. The predictions reach well beyond the agreement override in (\ref{ex:cgel-two-direction}): a noun in the class also shows a characteristic determiner frame (\mention{a lot of it} and totality \mention{the lot of it}, but not \ungram{some lot of it}) and restrictions on its complement (\mention{a number of protesters} but not \ungram{a number of money}), among the behaviours §\ref{sec:2} catalogues. The stabilizer is lexical inheritance: the class is small and conventional, and its members carry the behaviours as a package.

The substantive clause is \mention{remains accessible through $X$}. A case fits only if $X$ has internal structure whose default behaviour would have made $P$ inaccessible to $R$. Coordination (§\ref{sec:2-3}) is the textbook exclusion. In \mention{Bob and Jane are happy}, the plural agreement sums the conjuncts' features; no head's morphological number competes with them, so nothing was ever in a position to conceal $P$, and calling the construction \mention{transparent} would relabel summation as access. Everyday senses of \mention{transparent prose} or \mention{transparent intentions} are excluded too; the schema's slots have no values to bind.

This non-instance requirement is the umbrella's distinctive contribution. It keeps mediated accessibility from collapsing into a relabeling of familiar facts: the schema earns its place not by naming a kind but by supplying a relational diagnostic that a per-construction analysis lacks, one that separates genuine mediation from compositional summation and from everyday \term{clarity}. So \term{transparency}, as an umbrella, isn't itself projectible: applied on its own, the label projects nothing, and what projects is the particular profile it points to.

The paper therefore asks three questions. Which linguistic \mention{transparency} labels bind a non-trivial $X$-$P$-$R$ triple? Which triples support projection beyond the facts that motivated the label? What stabilizes the strongest profiles? The primary field is syntax, where the targets include agreement, syntactic-category licensing, complement selection, and behaviour under complement ellipsis. The answer is strongest for number-transparent QNs and transparent free relatives.

The English QN class illustrates the strongest case: once the class is recognized as small and conventional, membership predicts the whole cluster, not just the agreement override, reliably for core members and weakly at the edges (\mention{majority, minority}, sense-split \mention{couple}).

Transparent free relatives fill the slots differently. In \mention{What appear to be trousers are on the chair}, $X$ is the \mention{what}-clause, $P$ is the plural NP nucleus \mention{trousers}, and $R$ is matrix subject--verb agreement. In attributional cases, a second relation appears: the matrix accesses the nucleus's syntactic category through the \mention{what}-clause.

A typological extension (§\ref{sec:6}) then tests the topology of the schema beyond English: Spanish and Tsez keep the outward endpoint shape; Aleut, Lardil, Kayardild, and Chamorro vary the source and direction of access; and Algonquian/Kutenai obviation marks the boundary where a feature is relayed onto a head rather than accessed directly.

Projectibility is field-relative: a profile supports induction for a field's purposes, not for every inquiry at once. $X$ can be a lexical item, construction, intensional context, or derivationally complex word. $P$ can be a number/count feature, constituent meaning, referent, or stem representation. $R$ can be agreement, interpretation, substitution under co-reference, or masked priming. Accessibility itself can be class-like, as in core QNs, or gradient, as in compositionality ratings and priming magnitudes.

Our aim is constructive rather than exhaustive. The paper leaves aside most uses of \term{transparency} in linguistics, including extraction, islands, barriers, and phases; extraction-transparency is acknowledged in §\ref{sec:2} but not developed, and Chamorro wh-agreement appears in §\ref{sec:6} only as a topology comparison. It also leaves aside phonological or orthographic transparency and everyday talk of \mention{transparent explanation}. Referential or \mention{de re}/\mention{de dicto} transparency appears in §\ref{sec:3-ref} as the boundary case where the mediator-form structure stretches thinnest.

\term{Mediated accessibility} is more specific than \term{clarity} and broader than \term{compositionality}. Clarity is rhetorical: it doesn't single out a mediator or relation. Compositionality is the limiting case of form--meaning transparency, where a mediator's parts predict the whole. Number transparency is different: a QN's morphological number doesn't by itself predict the verb's number, and under the construction it doesn't have to.

Section 2 takes up morphosyntactic transparency, with number-transparent QNs and transparent free relatives as paradigms. Section 3 turns to form--meaning accessibility (constructional compositionality \autocite{goldberg-2006-constructions-at-work}, rated compound transparency \autocite{bell-schafer-2016-modelling-transparency}, and time-sensitive lexical processing \autocite{heyer-kornishova-2018-priming-eventually}), where the relation $R$ shifts from agreement to interpretation and processing, and notes referential transparency as the schema's boundary case; that family is developed in its own right in separate work.

Section 4 applies Haspelmath's \autocite*{haspelmath-2010-comparative-concepts} comparative-concept discipline to the schema's cross-linguistic ambitions and introduces the topology map. Section 5 names the stabilizers and treats Slater's stable property cluster (SPC) framework \autocite{slater-2015-natural-kindness} and Khalidi's causal-network account \autocite{khalidi-2018-causal-networks} as complementary diagnostics.
