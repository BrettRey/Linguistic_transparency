\section{Number-transparent NP empirical apparatus}

This appendix collects the COCA pilot data behind the §\ref{sec:2} claims about the number-transparent quantificational noun class. The body of the paper carries the qualitative claims; the audit trail lives here. It covers the two cluster behaviours with direct corpus data, the partitive/pseudo-partitive contrast (§\ref{a.2-partitive-vs.-pseudo-partitive-of-complements}) and the override direction (§\ref{sec:A-3}), plus the \mention{bunch} localization (§\ref{a.4-bunch-animate-vs.-inanimate-aggregates}) and the negative control (§\ref{sec:A-5}); the remaining cluster behaviours are \textit{CGEL}-coded predictions, not separately corpus-audited. Query specifications, aggregate counts, KWIC spot-checks, and coding notes are in the supplementary material (see Data availability).

\subsection{Methodology}

Queries were issued through the local Playwright wrapper at \path{tools/english-corpora/bin/ecorg.mjs} (May 2026). Each query is a surface string and the counts are raw COCA frequencies, filtered for head-versus-modifier use, idiom contamination, and sense only where noted. Two confounds recur and both inflate counts in the counter-predicted direction: a \term{modifier confound}, where N in X \mention{of} N is a modifier inside a larger NP (\mention{lots of day-care} for \mention{lots of day}), and a \term{parse-shift}, where the QN sits in a relative clause and the visible verb agrees with a different head (of 13 raw plural hits for \mention{a lot of money are}, 10 are parse-shifted, 1 is irrealis, 1 is contracted \mention{has}, and 1 is a genuine override).

Filtering was applied asymmetrically: counter-predicted cells were checked by hand more thoroughly than predicted ones, because the wrapper's KWIC mode was returning a Playwright error on every scripted call and checks had to be done manually and selectively. The counts below are attestations with a known bias in the paper's favour, not estimates of grammatical rates. The query specifications, raw returns, and per-cell coding decisions are in the supplementary material (see Data availability).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Partitive vs.~pseudo-partitive \mention{of}-complements}{Partitive vs. pseudo-partitive of-complements}}\label{a.2-partitive-vs.-pseudo-partitive-of-complements}

The contrast: \term{partitive} \mention{of}-complements have definite complements (\mention{of the money}, \mention{of those books}); \term{pseudo-partitive} \mention{of}-complements have bare or otherwise non-definite complements in the QN frame (\mention{of money}, \mention{of soldiers}, \mention{of some paintings}).

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Plural-only forms (\mention{lots, bags, heaps, loads, oodles, stacks, piles})}{Plural-only forms (lots, bags, heaps, loads, oodles, stacks, piles)}}

Pseudo-partitive (X of N): \mention{lots of people} 3,878; \mention{lots of money} 1,376; \mention{lots of time} 703; \mention{loads of money} 109; \mention{piles of money} 91; \mention{loads of people} 90; \mention{loads of time} 34; \mention{heaps of money} 15; \mention{heaps of people} 7; plus single-digit hits for several other combinations. \textbf{Total: 6,311.} KWIC checks of the single-digit cells show the expected mixture: \mention{lots of day} is modifier use (\mention{day-to-day}, \mention{day hikes}, \mention{day trips}); \mention{heaps of time} and \mention{piles of time} are genuine pseudo-partitive non-count-complement uses; \mention{piles of people} is literal piling rather than the quantificational use. \mention{Piles}, outside \textit{CGEL}'s [57] core inventory, patterns with the plural-only class and is included in the totals here.

Partitive (X of the N), hand-picked complements: \mention{lots of the time} 7; \mention{lots of the people} 5; \mention{loads of the day} 1, with 0 for \mention{piles} and \mention{heaps}. \textbf{Total: 13.} A wildcard sweep of the same frame (X \mention{of the *}, one query per form, July 2026) returns \mention{lots} 313 tokens across 235 distinct complements; \mention{stacks} 47/42; \mention{loads} 38/33; \mention{bags} 38/35; \mention{heaps} 14/14; \mention{piles} 12/12; \mention{oodles} 3/3. \textbf{Total: 465 across 374 distinct complements}, some thirty-six times the hand-picked count. The wildcard sweep attests the partitive frame for every member of the subgroup; a four-complement probe can't establish its absence.

The sweep reveals a sense split and generalizes the \mention{piles} result of §\ref{sec:2-4} across the whole subgroup. Wherever a member has a literal homonym, the determined frame recruits it: \mention{stacks} is dominated by library stacks and smokestacks (\mention{the library} 4, \mention{the feed} 2, \mention{the mill} 2, with singletons in \mention{the plant}, \mention{the power}, \mention{the coal}, \mention{the oil}, \mention{the factories}); \mention{heaps} by literal heaps and colliery spoil (\mention{the dead}, \mention{the brush}, \mention{the dross}, \mention{the Rhondda}); \mention{bags} and \mention{loads} by containers and cargo (\mention{the chips}, \mention{the greens}, \mention{the soil}, \mention{the biosolids}, \mention{the shellfish}). Even \mention{lots} carries a pharmaceutical batch sense (\mention{the drug} 3, \mention{the steroid} 2, \mention{the plasma}, \mention{the methylprednisolone}). \mention{Oodles}, the one member with no literal homonym, returns three hits and all three are quantificational (\mention{the cute}, \mention{the sheeple}, \mention{the stuff}). The clearly quantificational partitives concentrate on complements naming an already-established set: \mention{the same} (\mention{lots} 11, \mention{loads} 2), \mention{the stuff} (\mention{lots} 3, \mention{bags} 3, \mention{loads} 3, \mention{heaps} 1, \mention{oodles} 1), \mention{the latter}, \mention{the usual}, \mention{the other}, and definite human plurals (\mention{the women}, \mention{the people}, \mention{the kids}, \mention{the guests}, \mention{the members}, \mention{the children}). Sense assignment here is read off the complement types, not off inspected concordance lines, so it identifies where the literal reading concentrates without measuring its share.

Indefinite-NP complement (X of a N): \mention{lots of a day} 0; \mention{piles of a day} 0; \mention{heaps of a day} 0; \mention{loads of a day} 0; \mention{lots/piles/heaps/loads of an afternoon} 0. \textbf{Total: 0.} This null result covers eight specific strings and doesn't establish that the frame is unavailable; testing availability requires a wildcard sweep.

The wildcard numerator and the hand-picked pseudo-partitive denominator come from different complement inventories, so they don't support a partitive-share calculation. Dividing one by the other would measure the sampling rather than the language. The supported conclusion is qualitative: both frames are available across the subgroup, and the bare complement is by a wide margin the characteristic one.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\mention{Plenty}}{Plenty}}

Pseudo-partitive: \mention{plenty of time} 4,017; \mention{plenty of people} 1,563; \mention{plenty of money} 668; \mention{plenty of day} 4 (likely modifier). \textbf{Total: 6,252.}

Partitive: the two hand-picked complements return \mention{plenty of the time} 1 and \mention{plenty of the people} 1. The wildcard \mention{plenty of the *} returns \textbf{169 tokens across 140 distinct complements}, led by \mention{the latter} 7, \mention{the same} 6, \mention{the usual} 4, \mention{the things} 4, \mention{the tools} 3, \mention{the real} 3, \mention{the old} 3. \mention{Plenty} has no literal homonym, so the sense confound affecting \mention{stacks, heaps, bags, loads}, and \mention{piles} doesn't arise, and the count stands as quantificational partitive use. The wildcard result establishes productivity beyond the two hand-picked complements. No ratio is reported, for the reason given above.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\mention{Rest, remainder}}{Rest, remainder}}

Under the semantic criterion adopted in the body, \mention{rest} and \mention{remainder} aren't quantity nouns, which changes what the two frames mean for them. \textcite[271]{van-eynde-kim-2023-pseudo-partitives} define a pseudo-partitive as [N1 \mention{of} N2] in which \enquote{N1 denotes a quantity or amount of whatever it is that N2 denotes}, and treat the bare N2 as \enquote{characteristic} of the construction, the property by which it differs from a genuine partitive, \enquote{in which N2 is introduced by a definite determiner}. For \mention{lot} and the plural-only forms the semantic and syntactic criteria coincide. For \mention{rest}, they may come apart: a competing analysis could treat bare \mention{the rest of society} as pseudo-partitive by treating \mention{rest} as a part or amount expression. \mention{The remainder of time} remains what's left of time, not an amount of time, but the corpus doesn't by itself settle the lexical classification of \mention{rest}.

Under the semantic analysis, this subgroup has no pseudo-partitive use. The classification of \mention{rest} remains open because \mention{the rest of time} is grammatical and common, but its relevant tokens have a duration interpretation rather than a partitive one.

Bare complement (the X of N). Hand-picked complements returned \mention{the rest of time} 75; \mention{the rest of people} 12; \mention{the rest of day} 7; \mention{the remainder of time} 3; \mention{the remainder of day} 2; \mention{the rest of money} 1, total 100. A wildcard sweep returns far more: \mention{the rest of} [nn*] gives \textbf{2,820 tokens across 696 distinct bare complements} and \mention{the remainder of} [nn*] \textbf{126 across 96}. The leading complements are \mention{society} 442, \mention{humanity} 248, \mention{life} 150, \mention{nature} 78, \mention{mankind} 77, \mention{time} 75, \mention{eternity} 73, \mention{world} 63, \mention{creation} 42, \mention{Americans} 41, \mention{government} 31, \mention{baseball} 26, \mention{civilization} 26, \mention{Congress} 21.

Interpreting those totals requires sorting the sweep by what the complement denotes. Two classes emerge.

Where the complement denotes a \term{bounded unique whole}, the partitive reading is available and the determiner is unnecessary. Generic collectives are the productive case: \mention{society} 442, \mention{humanity} 248, \mention{nature} 78, \mention{mankind} 77, \mention{creation} 42, \mention{Americans} 41, \mention{government} 31, \mention{baseball} 26, \mention{civilization} 26, \mention{Congress} 21, and \mention{class} 15. Each denotes uniquely without an article and each has proper parts, so a complement subset is well defined: \mention{scientists and the rest of society}.

Where the complement denotes something \mention{unbounded}, it doesn't. Bare \mention{of time} offers nothing to partition, so \mention{the rest of time} isn't a partitive with a bare complement; it's a largely fixed duration expression meaning \enquote{all time henceforth} (\mention{for the rest of time}, \mention{till the rest of time}). \mention{Eternity} 73 patterns with it. On the genuine partitive reading the determiner is obligatory, so \ungram{\mention{the remainder of time}} stands as a starred datum once the reading is fixed. \mention{Life} 150 is ambiguous between the two classes (\enquote{the rest of one's life}, duration; \enquote{the rest of life on earth}, collective) and isn't counted for either.

The four hand-picked complements (\mention{day, time, people}, and \mention{money}) include no bounded generic collective. They sample only the class in which the bare complement fails, and their 100 tokens are duration uses, possessives, and modifier noise rather than partitives. The wildcard sweep identifies the productive class outside that sample.

Because [nn*] matches a string position, some hits are the first word of a longer nominal (\mention{the rest of day one}, \mention{the rest of tuition/books/housing}), and \mention{the rest of US} is mostly the country rather than the pronoun. The head of the distribution is unaffected.

Determined complement (the X of the N): \mention{the rest of the day} 1,932; \mention{the rest of the time} 830; \mention{the rest of the people} 272; \mention{the rest of the money} 222; \mention{the remainder of the day} 75; \mention{the remainder of the time} 21; \mention{the remainder of the money} 13. \textbf{Total: 3,365.} These are genuine partitives on both criteria.

Indefinite-NP complement: \mention{the remainder of a day} 1.

Determined-complement share \textit{on matched complements}: $3{,}365 / (3{,}365 + 100 + 1) \approx\! 97.1\%$ raw. Numerator and denominator draw on the same four complements, so the figure measures how often the whole carries a determiner within one construction for \mention{day, time, people}, and \mention{money}. It isn't a partitive-versus-pseudo-partitive ratio: the classification of \mention{rest} remains open, while \mention{remainder} is excluded under the semantic analysis. For those four complements the determiner is strongly preferred; the figure says nothing about the several hundred bare complements the sweep found.

The descriptive output is in the predicted direction at 98--100\% across all tested QN+complement combinations after filtering. The discriminating cells, where the head's number and the complement's diverge (\mention{a lot of people}, \mention{a number of people}, \mention{lots of money}, \mention{the rest of the people}), carry the evidential weight; the matched cells (\mention{a lot of money}, \mention{lots of people}, \mention{the rest of the money}) confirm the predicted direction but are equally consistent with head-driven agreement. Filtering was applied more thoroughly to counter-direction cells than to predicted-direction cells, so these figures are descriptive attestation, not estimated grammatical rates. \mention{A lot of money} required filtering: of 13 raw plural-agreement hits, 10 were parse-shifted (the surface string \mention{a lot of money are} appearing inside relative clauses modifying a plural-noun head, e.g., \mention{people who earn a lot of money are successful}; \mention{movies that make a lot of money are the biggest help}; \mention{times when we\ldots{} make a lot of money are gone}), 1 was the irrealis \mention{were} (\mention{if a lot of money were at stake}), and 1 \mention{'s} hit was singular \mention{has} (matches prediction); only 1 hit was a genuine plural override (\mention{a lot of money are being raised}). Filtered count: 1 plural / 90 singular = 98.9\% singular.

The other checked counter-direction cells are artefacts. \mention{A number of people is/was} is headed by \mention{condition}, \mention{suggestion}, \mention{ability}, or \mention{counsel}, not by \mention{number}; \mention{lots of people is/was} is headed by expressions such as \mention{murder}, \mention{hauling}, or \mention{disappearance}, with one non-standard existential hit; and \mention{lots of money are} occurs inside a PP modifying plural \mention{men}. The large \mention{a lot of people is/was} cell remains unfiltered, so its 98.0\% descriptive row is conservative.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\mention{Bunch}: animate vs.~inanimate aggregates}{Bunch: animate vs. inanimate aggregates}}\label{a.4-bunch-animate-vs.-inanimate-aggregates}

Testing \textit{CGEL}'s claim (§18.2, p.~503) that \mention{bunch} tilts collective for inanimate aggregates and transparent for groups of people. Animate \mention{bunch}-subjects are consistently plural across \mention{a bunch of people} and \mention{a bunch of kids}, supporting the transparent half. The inanimate half gets no positive support: inanimate \mention{bunch} is rare in subject position, its typical use being as object; \textit{CGEL}'s own example \mention{A bunch of flowers was presented to the teacher} returns no hits; and the few inanimate-subject tokens take plural rather than the predicted singular. Counts are small enough that this bears only on whether the contrast projects as a corpus regularity, not on the grammatical claim, and the full returns are in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Negative control: referential \mention{the number of}}\label{sec:A-5}

If the override tracked the quantificational construction rather than the noun \mention{number} or \mention{N}-of-\mention{N} configurations in general, then referential \mention{the number of}, where \mention{number} is the head rather than a quantifier, should agree head-driven (singular) even with a plural complement. A COCA check (June 2026) bears this out on the same lexeme. \mention{The number of people} occurs with \mention{is}/\mention{are} at 3/1 and \mention{the number of cases} at 20/1: head-driven singular, with plural only at attraction level. \mention{A number of people} occurs at 0/52 and \mention{a number of cases} at 0/3: categorical plural override. The counts are small, since the bare subject-verb adjacency is rare, but the direction is unambiguous and the contrast sits on one noun: \mention{number} overrides under \mention{a} and not under \mention{the}. The override is a property of the quantificational construction, not of the lexeme or of \mention{N}-of-\mention{N} structure as such.
