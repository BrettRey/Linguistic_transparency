Linguistic transparency is best understood as a family of mediated accessibility relations, not a unitary syntactic property, captured by an umbrella schema: \mention{$X$ is transparent with respect to property $P$ for relation $R$ when $P$, which $X$ might otherwise have concealed from $R$, remains accessible through $X$ for the purposes of $R$}. The schema is a comparative concept in Haspelmath's sense, an analyst's question askable of any language, not a claim that any language has a descriptive category of transparency.

The schema locates \term{projectibility profiles}: cases where an observable base licenses reliable inference to further, unobserved properties (projectibility, in Goodman's sense), stabilized by an identifiable source. The contribution is twofold: the comparative concept makes that question askable of any language, and a non-instance requirement, that the structure could otherwise have concealed the property, gives the relation empirical bite, separating mediated access from compositional summation (coordination is excluded). A transparency label licenses no projection on its own; what projects is the specific profile it picks out. Projectibility is field-relative, so the paper keeps the fields apart.

The primary results are syntactic. English number-transparent quantificational nouns are the strongest profile: a small, stable class whose membership predicts agreement override, determiner patterns, partitive availability, fused-head behaviour, count/non-count percolation, and restricted premodification, stabilized by lexical convention. Transparent free relatives are a second profile.

Spanish and Tsez extend the outward endpoint topology beyond English, while Aleut gives a qualified positive for null-source subpart access; Lardil, Kayardild, and Chamorro show inward and path-distributed topologies; and Algonquian/Kutenai obviation marks the boundary where a feature is relayed rather than directly accessed. These cases pressure-test the schema rather than displacing the English profiles as the worked core.

Beyond syntax, form--meaning accessibility (constructional compositionality, compound transparency, and morphological priming) gives three within-domain profiles whose cross-domain integration is left to separate work. Against stable-property-cluster and causal-network accounts of kinds, the paper extends projectibility-first analysis to a relational concept; kindhood is a downstream verdict on profiles that earn it, not the master question of the analysis.
