This paper analyzes linguistic transparency as a family of mediated accessibility relations across syntax and form--meaning mapping. A structure is transparent when a property remains accessible through a grammatical or representational mediator for some relation, despite structure that might otherwise have concealed it. The schema's distinctive move is a non-instance requirement: a configuration counts only where the mediator's own default behaviour could have concealed the property, which excludes mere feature summation such as coordination. The schema is a comparative concept: it asks which local profiles support reliable projection from observable configurations to less visible grammatical properties, and kind-like behaviour belongs to those profiles, not to the umbrella.

The worked cases are English number-transparent quantificational nouns and transparent free relatives. Spanish and Tsez extend the same outward-endpoint pattern; Aleut, Lardil, Kayardild, and Chamorro pressure-test the direction and distribution of the relation; and Algonquian/Kutenai obviation marks the boundary where an intervening head acquires a feature of its own, so the relation tracks that rather than the bearer's. Korean dative subjects test the schema where the candidate default is case marking rather than agreement, and the verdict there stays open for reasons internal to Korean. Form--meaning accessibility, including constructional compositionality, compound transparency, and morphological priming, extends the schema beyond syntax, with cross-domain integration left as an open programme. The result is a field-relative model in which grammatical kindhood is a downstream verdict of projectibility, not an inherent structural feature.
