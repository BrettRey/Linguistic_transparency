Linguistic transparency is best understood as a family of mediated accessibility relations, not a unitary syntactic property, captured by an umbrella schema: \mention{$X$ is transparent with respect to property $P$ for relation $R$ when $P$, which $X$ might otherwise have concealed from $R$, remains accessible through $X$ for the purposes of $R$}. The schema is a comparative concept in Haspelmath's sense, an analyst's question askable of any language, not a claim that any language has a descriptive category of transparency.

The schema locates \term{projectibility profiles}: cases where an observable base licenses reliable inference to further, unobserved properties (projectibility, in Goodman's sense), stabilized by an identifiable source. The contribution is twofold: the comparative concept makes that question askable of any language, and a non-instance requirement, that the structure could otherwise have concealed the property, gives the relation empirical bite, separating mediated access from compositional summation (coordination is excluded). Projectibility is field-relative, so the paper keeps the fields apart.

The primary results are syntactic. English number-transparent quantificational nouns are the strongest profile: a small, stable class whose membership predicts agreement override, determiner patterns, partitive availability, fused-head behaviour, count/non-count percolation, and restricted premodification, stabilized by lexical convention. Transparent free relatives are a second profile. The same question recurs in Spanish and, in a head-marking language, Tsez; Korean case-stacking and scrambling is the deeper prospective test, left to forthcoming co-authored work.

Beyond syntax, form--meaning accessibility (constructional compositionality, compound transparency, and morphological priming) gives three within-domain profiles whose cross-domain integration is left to separate work. Against stable-property-cluster and causal-network accounts of kinds, the paper extends projectibility-first analysis to a relational concept; kindhood is a downstream verdict on profiles that earn it, not the master question of the analysis.
