# Terminology

From `PROJECT-BRIEF.md` and inferred decisions. These are decisions, not suggestions.

## Technical terms

- ***transparency*** — italicised on first mention, and italicised whenever used as the name of the concept (rather than as part of an ordinary noun phrase like "the transparency of the mediator").
- ***mediated accessibility*** — the umbrella concept; technical term.
- **mediator** — the X in the umbrella schema.
- **profile** — a particular pattern of transparency relations ("transparency profile").
- **local kind** — used in the Khalidi (or maintenance-view) sense; a kind whose grounding is in a particular causal network rather than a fundamental physical kind.
- **comparative concept** vs. **descriptive category** — Haspelmath's distinction; used in §6 and §7.
- **constructicon** — CxG term for the network of constructions; used in §3.
- **field-relative projectibility** — used in §7 if the metaphysical-framework decision aligns with `Field_relative_HPC_categories/`.

## Names and abbreviations

- **CGEL** — *The Cambridge Grammar of the English Language* (Huddleston & Pullum 2002). Introduced on first use; thereafter as ordinary type.
- **CxG** — Construction Grammar. Introduced on first use; thereafter as ordinary type.
- **HPC** — homeostatic property cluster. Introduced on first use; used sparingly in this paper since the framework choice may shift.

## Italicisation

- Italicise mentioned forms: *a lot of*, *plenty of*, *paleness*, *business*, *busy*, *blackbird*.
- Italicise mentioned constructions: *a lot of N*, *the team are*.
- House style: `\mention{}` macro in eventual LaTeX.

## Predicate vs. nominalised use

- "X is transparent with respect to P" — predicate use, preferred when the umbrella schema is being applied.
- "the transparency of X" — nominalised, used sparingly.
- "transparency profile" — used as a noun phrase to refer to a particular pattern.

## Antonym

- **opacity / opaque** — used as the principal antonym. Avoid "non-transparent."
- **encapsulation / encapsulated** — used in the thesis statement; treat as adjacent rather than synonymous to opacity. **Decision pending** (per `DECISIONS.md` Open) on whether the paper makes a substantive distinction between opacity and encapsulation, or treats them as interchangeable.

## Avoid

- "It is important to note that"
- "This paper seeks to"
- "complex and multifaceted"
- "not merely X but Y" as a repeated frame
- inflated claims about "challenging traditional paradigms"
- "clarity" as a synonym for transparency
- "regularity" as a synonym for transparency
- "non-transparent" (use *opaque*)
- everyday senses: "transparent explanation," "transparent terminology," "transparent pedagogy"

## Prefer

- contractions
- ~60-word paragraphs
- the umbrella schema's vocabulary (X / P / R / mediator / accessibility) when reframing examples
- direct verbs
- concrete contrasts among the senses
- examples that make the mediator visible
- "field-relative projectibility" and "local kind" as technical terms
- measured claims about which transparency profiles are projectible
