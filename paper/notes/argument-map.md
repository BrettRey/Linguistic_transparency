# Argument map

Distilled from `PROJECT-BRIEF.md`. Use as a single-page reference while drafting.

## The umbrella schema (single load-bearing piece)

> X is transparent with respect to property P for relation R when P remains accessible through X for the purposes of R.

Every case study in the paper must be re-expressible in this form. If a case can't be re-expressed, either it isn't really transparency in the article's sense, or the schema needs revision.

## One-paragraph thesis

Linguistic transparency is best understood not as a unitary syntactic property but as a family of mediated accessibility relations. A construction, expression, or grammatical configuration is transparent when information that might otherwise be encapsulated remains available for agreement, interpretation, constructional generalisation, processing, or cross-linguistic comparison. The umbrella is not a natural kind. Some local transparency profiles, including CGEL number-transparent quantifier constructions, CxG constructional compositionality gradients, and morphologically transparent word families with priming effects, may be projectible local kinds.

## Senses included vs. demoted

**Included as senses (1 umbrella + 4 substantive + 2 supporting):**

1. Mediated accessibility (umbrella)
2. Morphosyntactic transparency (substantive core)
3. Constructional transparency (substantive centre)
4. Semantic transparency (substantive extension)
5. Processing transparency (supporting: cognitive realism)
6. Typological transparency (supporting: comparative-concept discipline)

**Demoted or excluded:**

- Extraction / island transparency — historically influential subtype only, not the centre.
- Phonological / orthographic transparency — analogue, not core case.
- "Transparent explanation," "transparent terminology," "transparent pedagogy" — rhetoric/pedagogy, not the article's topic.

## What would falsify or weaken the argument

- **If the umbrella schema can't accommodate a clear case of "transparency" in linguistics**, the umbrella claim weakens. Likely problem cases: extraction, phase-based opacity, Condition C effects. The schema must either accommodate them as restricted subtypes or the article must explicitly exclude them.
- **If none of the candidate local kinds (CGEL number transparency, CxG compositionality gradients, morphological-priming transparency) survive the projectibility check**, the local-kinds claim collapses. The processing section (§5) is the load-bearing empirical evidence; if the priming effects don't replicate or if the literature is contested, §7's local-kind candidacy weakens.
- **If the brief's seven citations don't survive verification**, several sections rest on missing foundations. The Bell & Schäfer and Heyer & Kornishova references are the most likely failure points.
- **If the sister paper `Field_relative_HPC_categories/` settles on a metaphysical framework that conflicts with the brief's Khalidian framing**, this paper inherits a consistency problem with its sister.
- **If the paper drifts into "transparency is a useful descriptive label,"** the contribution claim collapses. The non-negotiable argumentative-discipline list is the diagnostic.

## What strengthens the argument

- Cleanly source-grounded *a lot of* contrast in the introduction.
- One clear empirical case in §5 where transparency affects processing in a replicated way.
- The structural analogue with the HPC book chapter 7 NPI passage (NPIs pass the cluster check but fail the homeostasis check). The same argument shape applies to *transparency* as an umbrella: it passes a shallow projectibility check at the umbrella level but fails the homeostasis check, so the kind-talk has to be relocated to the local profiles.
- Explicit acknowledgement of comparative-concept status in §6, defusing the universal-smuggling objection.
- Connection across sister papers (`What_do_we_mean_by_language/`, `Field_relative_HPC_categories/`, HPC book): the same metaphysical move applied to a relational concept.
