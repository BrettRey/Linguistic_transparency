# Cross-linguistic leads: quantified-subject / mediated-access transparency

Status as of 2026-06-16. Source-grounding rule: nothing here goes into a paper until read in the primary or a page-locatable secondary source. The `citeturn…` markers in the original deep-research report are NOT checkable citations; treat the whole report as leads.

## The schema test these leads must pass

A case is a transparency *instance* (not a non-instance) only if the language has a **within-language conceal variant**: a default that would route agreement to the quantifier head / a default form, overridden in the marked case. "Verb agrees with the semantic plural" is NOT enough on its own — that can be plain head agreement (a non-instance, like coordination). The discriminating fact is whether the *concealing* variant is attested.

## Verified

- **Tsez (head-marking; in the paper, §6).** Long-distance agreement: matrix verb agrees class IV with the clause (default/conceal) OR class III with the embedded absolutive (transparent). Minimal pair attested in the same sentence. Source read: Potsdam & Polinsky 1999, WCCFL 18: 434–447, p. 436 (`literature/potsdam-polinsky-1999-tsez-lda-wccfl18.pdf`); fuller: Polinsky & Potsdam 2001, NLLT 19(3): 583–646. **Used in the paper.**
- **Spanish (nod, in the paper, §6).** *la mayoría de los manifestantes gritaban* (pl, complement) ~ *gritaba* (sg, *mayoría*); pl more frequent, obligatory under a plural predicative. Source: RAE, *Español al día* (page identified; verbatim text not pulled — site is JS-rendered and 403s bots — but the fact is textbook and the page title is the example). Used only as a one-line nod, not a full interlinear.

## Checked and REJECTED

- **Swahili `idadi kubwa ya watoto wanacheza` (NOT used).** Verb agrees class 2 with *watoto*, but Zerbian & Krifka (*Quantification across Bantu languages*, §2.4 ex. 32a; `literature/zerbian-krifka-bantu-quantification.pdf`) give **no class-9 concealing variant**, and their §2.5 analysis treats *idadi kubwa ya* as a grammaticalized prenominal quantifier (Spec-DP), making *watoto* the head and class-2 agreement plain head agreement. Fails the conceal-variant test → non-instance on present evidence. Northern Sotho *seripagare* (33d) is the opposite pole (verb agrees class 7 with 'half'), and Z&K note it "is primarily not used for quantification." Bantu does not give a clean instance from this source.

## Unverified leads for the Korean / cross-linguistic extension (verify before any use)

The report's transparent/partial/non-transparent gradient. Read each source AND check for the within-language conceal variant.

- **Hebrew** (transparent): *mispar našim panu* 'a number of women applied'; verb plural with the quantified noun. Source to read: Francez & Goldring, "Quantifiers in Modern Hebrew," ch. 7 §7.2.1 ex. 25a. Check whether singular agreement with *mispar* is also attested (conceal variant).

- **Turkish** (mostly non-transparent): *birçok kişi … düşünmez* (sg). Source: Göksel & Kerslake §12.2.2.2.

## Framing points worth keeping (conceptual, safe)

- English is not typologically quirky: quantified subjects expose multiple potential controllers across languages; what varies is how freely the grammar lets the plural set-denoting element surface on the verb. Independent statement of the §6 comparative-concept thesis. (Partly folded into §6 already.)
- Keep grammatical transparency distinct from agreement **attraction** (Bock & Miller: attraction is an error; the QN override is a grammatical option). The COCA appendix already filters attraction-like parse-shift cases. Could make the distinction explicit in §2.4 with a Bock & Miller cite — optional sharpening, not yet done.
- Corbett's Agreement Hierarchy ("greater semantic justification" rightward) is a related framework; probably scope creep for this paper, relevant for the extension.

- **Pertensive marking** (NP-internal head marking of possession) does not predict clause-level quantified-subject transparency — different grammatical domain. The report independently reaches this. Thread closed.
